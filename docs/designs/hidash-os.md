## Problem Statement

hidash.in is Muhammed Hidash's personal brand site. He is CTO at Dentobees, and his brand line is "Startup builder. Tech by craft, business by curiosity." Today the site is a macOS-style desktop with VS Code windows on desktop and an iPhone-style home screen on mobile, plus a server-rendered SEO blog at `/blog`.

Copying macOS more faithfully would not make it memorable. Framer alone sells at least six macOS portfolio templates. The goal is a site that LinkedIn visitors, most of them on phones, find so delightful that they share a screenshot, a recording or a score on their first visit. It must also read as the work of a world-class engineer.

## What Makes This Cool

- **Hidash OS.** It is his own operating system with Apple-level craft: an "H" monogram instead of the Apple logo, his own wallpaper and icons, and a full boot sequence.
- **He lives inside it.** A stylized mini-Hidash (a Rive character) reacts to what the visitor does.
- **Bug Hunt.** A 60-second game on the live OS. Bugs crawl out of real windows on desktop, and out of icons and widgets on the phone. Each bug is an engineering shortcut he guards against. Catching one shows how he handles it. A recap links to the build or note that proves it. The game ends in a poster-style share card: "I debugged Hidash OS · 47 bugs".

## Constraints

- The audience is mainly mobile, inside LinkedIn's in-app browser (a webview) on iOS and Android. Every key moment must work there.
- Visual quality must be Mac-grade. The user rejected flat or "kiddy" illustration.
- No Apple intellectual property: no Apple logo, icons, wallpapers, bundled SF Pro font files or Apple UI kits. The UI uses the browser's system font stack.
- Apple product names (Spotlight, Dock, Liquid Glass, App Store) appear only in this doc and in code. On screen, Hidash OS uses its own words: Search, Dock (a generic word), Glass, Builds.
- `/blog` and the server-rendered home summary plus `Person` structured data must keep working for SEO.
- Stack stays as is: Next.js 16 (App Router), React 19, Tailwind 4, framer-motion.
- The user wants lanes built in parallel, fast.

## Premises (agreed)

1. Mac/iPhone polish is the minimum, not the wow. The wow is his character, his OS identity, his game and his real work.
2. It is "Hidash OS": Apple-level craft with its own identity. It never pretends to be macOS.
3. The audience is LinkedIn visitors, mostly on phones. Mobile must be as impressive as desktop and fast. Success means a share on the first visit.
4. The desktop character is a stylized likeness, not a photoreal deepfake. The Live AI Twin comes later.
5. Bug Hunt is the flagship game: 60 seconds, played on the live OS, ending in a share card that links back.
6. The wow must not cost speed or SEO.

### Shell selection, routing and history

- **Which shell renders:**
  - A small inline script in `<head>` runs before the first paint. It sets `data-shell="phone"` when `matchMedia('(pointer: coarse)').matches` and the viewport's short side is below 768px. Otherwise it sets `data-shell="desktop"`.
  - Tablets get the desktop shell with 44px touch targets.
  - The server renders a neutral frame (wallpaper plus the SEO summary), so nothing visibly swaps at hydration.
  - The shell is chosen once per page load. Resizing or rotating doesn't swap it. The phone shell supports landscape layout.
- **History:** the URL is the single source of truth for what's open.
  - **Open:** opening an app or window pushes an entry. The URL carries the open set, for example `?app=builds&item=crm` on the phone, or `?w=builds,notes&focus=notes` on desktop.
  - **Close:** closing from the UI (home bar drag, "‹ Back", window close) computes the resulting state. It calls `history.back()` only when the previous entry (tracked in an in-memory stack of OS-pushed URLs) exactly equals that resulting state. Otherwise it calls `replaceState` with the resulting set.
    - Example: open Builds, open Notes, close Builds. The result is Notes only, so it uses `replaceState` and Notes stays open.
  - **Minimize** isn't navigation. It's geometry state in the store, and the URL is unchanged.
  - **Back and forward:** `popstate` re-renders the OS to match the URL. Pressing back always undoes the most recent open.
  - **Deep links:** a deep-link landing (`?app=…`) first inserts a base `/` entry with `replaceState` + `pushState`. The first back returns to the home screen instead of leaving the site. Deep links skip the boot.
  - This covers the Android system back gesture inside LinkedIn's webview.
- **Notes:**
  - Notes renders posts inside its own window or app, from the same `data/posts.ts` the `/blog` pages use (no iframe).
  - Each post has an "Open as web page" link to `/blog/<slug>`.
  - `/blog` pages stay as they are (canonical, server-rendered), plus an "Open in Hidash OS" link back to `/?app=notes&item=<slug>`.

### Architecture

The current WindowManager context, react-rnd and the Monaco editor are all replaced.

- **OS store (`lib/os/store.ts`, zustand):**
  - Windows: id, app id, position and size, z-order, and state (normal, minimized or zoomed).
  - Which window has focus, and which app is running.
  - **Spawn surfaces:** each shell registers on-screen rectangles through a `useSpawnSurface(ref, kind)` hook (`kind`: window, icon, widget). The store keeps their current positions. Bug Hunt reads only `store.spawnSurfaces`.
  - **Event bus:** `os.emit('boot:end' | 'app:open' | 'app:close' | 'window:focus' | 'idle' | 'game:start' | 'game:catch' | 'game:end' | 'share:click', payload)`. `share:click` carries `{channel}`. Inside the OS, the character and analytics only listen to the bus. The share page (`/bug-hunt/s/[token]`) runs outside the OS and calls the analytics client directly.
  - **Shell code:** each shell (desktop and phone) is its own lazy chunk, loaded after the inline script picks it. The phone never downloads desktop shell code.
  - **Lifecycle state (eng review):**
    - `os.lifecycle` holds sticky facts: `booted`, `bootSkipped` (deep link), `firstVisit` and `gameActive`.
    - A subscriber that loads late (the character, analytics) reads `os.lifecycle` when it mounts, before subscribing, so it never misses `boot:end`.
    - Deep links set `booted` with `bootSkipped: true`.
  - **Spawn rectangles:** a non-reactive registry (`Map`) read by the game loop each frame. It is not stored in React state, so dragging a window doesn't re-render the whole OS.
  - **The open set** comes from the URL (`parseOsUrl`). The zustand store holds only geometry, z-order and minimized flags keyed by app id, so there's one owner per piece of state.
- **App list (`apps/<id>/manifest.ts`):** each app declares its name, icon, default size, menus, and a lazily loaded desktop view and phone view. Both views read the shared content.
- **Desktop shell:**
  - Menu bar showing the focused app's menus.
  - Dock with a magnification curve, launch bounce and running-app dots.
  - Desktop icons and the windows themselves.
  - Search, opened with ⌘K / Ctrl+K or the search icon in the menu bar.
  - Widgets.
- **Windows:** custom pointer-based dragging and resizing from every edge, double-click the title bar to zoom, and grey traffic lights when inactive.
  - **Minimize** is transform-only: the window scales and moves into its Dock icon, with a soft fade.
  - No genie effect.
- **Keyboard:**
  - Only shortcuts a web page can capture: ⌘K / Ctrl+K for Search, Esc to close Search and menus or end a Bug Hunt round, and arrow keys in lists.
  - No app switcher.
  - Menus show commands without OS-reserved shortcuts (no ⌘W, ⌘Q, ⌘M, ⌘H, ⌘N, ⌘Tab or ⌘Space).
- **Phone shell:**
  - Home screen with swipeable pages, widgets, and a dock holding apps that aren't on the grid.
  - Apps zoom open from their icon (framer-motion `layoutId`).
- **Phone gestures:**
  - The in-app home bar sits above the safe area (`bottom: calc(env(safe-area-inset-bottom) + 8px)`) with a 44px-high hit area. Dragging up on it closes the app.
  - There is no edge-swipe back, because browsers use that gesture.
  - Every app shows a visible "‹ Back" or close control in its navigation bar.
  - All of this is verified inside LinkedIn's webview on iOS and Android.
- **Apps:** Journey, Builds (App Store-style product pages), About, Beliefs, Notes, Say Hi (LinkedIn-first) and Bug Hunt. Monaco is removed.
- **Web app manifest:** included as a cheap extra for visitors in a full browser, since LinkedIn's webview doesn't offer "Add to Home Screen".

### Performance tiers (glass and effects)

- **Tier A (default):**
  - At most 3 surfaces use live `backdrop-filter`: the menu bar or status area, the dock, and the focused window.
  - Unfocused windows and widgets fake glass by showing the pre-blurred wallpaper image, fixed in place, under a tint.
- **Tier B (low end):**
  - Chosen when `navigator.deviceMemory ≤ 4`, or `hardwareConcurrency ≤ 4`, or a frame monitor measures an average frame time above 20ms over 1s.
  - All surfaces use the pre-blurred image or solid colors, with no live blur.
- **Device tier vs glass mode:**
  - The **device tier** (A or B) comes from the detection rule above and doesn't change during a session, except a downgrade from the frame monitor.
  - The **glass mode** follows the device tier, except during a Bug Hunt round, when it's forced to B.
  - Effects that depend on hardware (for example the rain finale) are gated on the device tier, not the glass mode.
- **Reduced transparency:**
  - `prefers-reduced-transparency` switches to solid surfaces where the browser supports it.
  - A "Reduce transparency" toggle in Settings (inside the menu) covers WebKit webviews that don't report it.

### Boot and first visit

- **Boot (user decision: classic full-screen boot):** monogram plus progress bar.
- **Timing:**
  - It lasts at least 1.2s and at most 2.5s.
  - It ends when the shell's ready signal fires (hydrated, and the wallpaper decoded) or at the 2.5s cap, whichever comes first after the minimum.
  - If the shell isn't ready at the cap, the boot gives way to the wallpaper plus a skeleton dock and home screen until it is.
  - The boot isn't skippable.
- **First visit detection:**
  - The same inline `<head>` script reads a cookie `hos_seen` (falling back to localStorage) and sets `data-boot="on"` or `"off"` before the first paint. The page stays static.
  - The cookie is set to last 1 year when the boot ends.
  - If the LinkedIn webview has cleared storage, a returning visitor sees the boot again. This is accepted, and capped at 2.5s.
  - Deep links (`?app=`) and the share page never show the boot.
- **From boot to the first round:**
  - When the boot ends, the character greets the visitor on both shells: "Hey! I'm Hidash. Want to see what I'm shipping?" with **[Show me]** and **[Play Bug Hunt]**.
  - On the phone, the first widget row is the Bug Hunt widget (with a Play button) next to the profile widget, above the fold.

### Character (mini-Hidash)

  - The WASM file is self-hosted, matched to the package version and served as `application/wasm`, so nothing is fetched from unpkg.
  - It loads after the first interaction, or after 3s idle following `boot:end`.
  - Until then, and if loading fails, the character is a static SVG pose.
- **Launch scope (eng review):**
  - Required for launch: idle, wave and celebrate.
  - The other states ship as the art is approved.
  - The character lane must deliver one approved animated state (wave) as a proof gate before more states are produced.
  - Hidash approves the likeness.
- **Art direction:** a refined, stylized likeness built from his reference video and photos, not cartoonish. The canvas SVG is only a placeholder.
- **Desktop:**
  - Sits on the dock and greets visitors.
  - "Show me" in v1 is a single step: he walks to the Builds icon and points, then Builds opens on the WhatsApp AI Sales CRM. Any click cancels it.
  - Sleeps after 30s idle.
- **Phone:**
  - Lives only on the home screen, at the right edge just above the dock, 64px tall.

### Bug Hunt

- **Starting and input:**
  - A round starts from the widget, the dock or app icon, or the character's button.
  - On the phone, any open app closes first, so the round plays on the home screen.
  - During a round the OS is locked: windows can't be dragged and apps can't open. The game layer captures all pointer input, and a tap on empty space counts as a miss.
  - Esc or a Pause/End button ends the round and shows the recap.
  - Switching away (`visibilitychange` to hidden) pauses the round.
  - Starting a round pushes `?app=bughunt`. A `popstate` during a round (Android system back or browser back) ends the round into the recap, which then has its own back entry. Back never leaves the site mid-round.
- **Desktop setup:** before a desktop round starts, if fewer than two windows are open, Builds and Journey open in cascade so bugs have real windows to crawl out of.
- **Spawning:** bugs spawn from `store.spawnSurfaces`.
  - Desktop: window edges, dock icons, desktop icons and widgets.
  - Phone: icon and widget rectangles.
  - At most 6 bugs on screen on the phone and 10 on desktop. Tap targets are 44px.
- **Bug types and lessons:**
  - Lessons are new content in `data/bugs.ts`, written by Hidash in contract 0 (Claude drafts them, he approves).
  - Each lesson is one sentence on how he prevents that shortcut, plus a link to the strongest existing proof:

    | Bug | Link |
    |---|---|
    | TODO in production | Software Factory build: QA agent + holdout tests |
    | Skipped tests | Software Factory build |
    | Hardcoded secret | Software Factory build: secrets in the Keychain |
    | Works on my machine | Software Factory build: Docker sandbox |
    | Copy-paste code | Note: "Velocity Is a Lie If It's Built on Technical Debt" |
    | Unvalidated AI output | Note: "Velocity Is a Lie…" |

  - A lesson with no proof shows text only.
- **Lesson timing:**
  - During play, a catch shows a short, non-blocking toast (the bug's name and a one-line lesson, about 2.5s, no links). The timer never pauses.
  - Links appear only in the end-of-round recap, which lists each bug type caught with its lesson and link.
- **Finale:**
  - Device tier A with WebGL available (even though glass is forced to B during the round): in the last 10s, rain droplets bead on the glass and a wiper sweeps it clear at zero. This is a WebGL refraction shader, loaded only with Bug Hunt.
  - Otherwise: a static rain overlay and a fade.
  - Reduced motion: bugs appear and fade in place instead of crawling, with no rain or wiper and a static finale.
- **Accessibility:**
  - Bugs are `<button aria-label="Bug: TODO in production">` elements. Tab moves to the nearest bug, and Enter catches it.
  - In keyboard mode bugs move 40% slower.
  - A live region announces catches and the time left.
  - A "Read the lessons" link opens the recap without playing.

### Share

- **Share token:**
  - **Round start:** `POST /api/bug-hunt/start` returns a start nonce, `{startedAt}` signed with HMAC.
  - **Round end:** the client posts the nonce, its score and its active (unpaused) play time to `POST /api/bug-hunt/result`.
  - **Plausibility bound:** the server checks the signature, that active time ≤ 65s and ≤ (now − startedAt), and that the score ≤ maximum spawn rate × active time (the cap is a constant in `data/bugs.ts`).
  - This is a plausibility bound, not proof of fair play.
  - Rounds that were paused or ended early still get a token with their score, bounded by their active time.
  - It returns a token: base64url of `{s: score 0–200, t: date, m: bitmask of bug types caught}` plus an HMAC-SHA256 signature using the `SHARE_SECRET` environment variable.
  - The token carries no free text and no names.
- **`/bug-hunt/s/[token]`:**
  - The server verifies the signature and bounds.
  - **Valid token:** renders a poster-style score card, "I debugged Hidash OS · 47 bugs", with the caught lessons and a **[Beat 47 — Play Bug Hunt]** button. The button opens `/?app=bughunt&challenge=47` and starts a round directly, with no boot.
  - **Invalid token:** a generic Bug Hunt card with a Play button (HTTP 200).
  - The Open Graph image comes from `next/og` and uses only the verified numbers.
- **Sharing:**
  - When `navigator.share` exists, it's offered first.
  - Otherwise: a LinkedIn share link (`https://www.linkedin.com/sharing/share-offsite/?url=…`) plus copy-link.
  - Every share URL carries `utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=share&utm_campaign=bughunt`.

### Quality gates

- **JavaScript budget:**
  - The phone path's initial JavaScript for `/` must be ≤ 220 KB gzipped. That's the framework and shared chunks, the page entry, and the phone-shell chunk.
  - Enforced in CI with `size-limit`, using entries resolved from the build manifest (not the `next build` printout, which may not report this in Next 16).
  - The desktop shell, character, game, WebGL and app views are lazy chunks outside the budget.
  - Contract 0 checks that framer-motion `LazyMotion` with `domMax` (needed for `layoutId`) fits.
  - **Second budget (eng review):**
    - Total bytes transferred on a phone first visit, up to "home interactive", must be ≤ 600 KB (compressed). That includes the wallpaper AVIF, fonts and anything loaded automatically.
    - Measured by a Playwright test with request capture at 390×844.
    - Rive and the game are excluded only because they load after interaction or idle.
- **Speed:**
  - Largest Contentful Paint ≤ 2.5s on a mid-range Android phone over 4G (Lighthouse mobile preset).
  - Frame time ≤ 16.7ms during app launches in Tier A, and ≤ 20ms in Tier B.
- **Photos:** converted to AVIF/WebP with blurred placeholders.
- **Fidelity checklist:**
  - Measured from screenshots and screen recordings of Hidash's own Mac and iPhone, not Apple's UI kits.
  - Tolerances: corner radii ±1px, spacing ±2px, tap targets ≥ 44px, animation durations ±30ms, no visible overshoot beyond the reference.
  - Hidash signs off component by component.
  - Deliberate differences: the monogram, wallpaper, icons, palette, on-screen names and Bug Hunt.
- **Accessibility:** full keyboard support for the OS, real buttons and links, reduced motion and transparency honored (with the Settings toggle), and Bug Hunt's keyboard mode.
- **Testing:**
  - Playwright screenshot tests at 1440×900 (desktop) and 390×844 (phone).
  - Manual checks inside LinkedIn's in-app browser on iOS and Android.

## Success Criteria

- On a mid-range Android phone inside LinkedIn's in-app browser, the home screen is interactive within 3s on a first visit (boot included) and within 1.5s on a return visit while the `hos_seen` cookie persists.
- Bug Hunt plays end to end inside LinkedIn's in-app browser on iOS and Android, and a share link previews the verified score card on LinkedIn.
- Every component on the fidelity checklist meets its tolerances, signed off by Hidash.
- `/blog` keeps a Lighthouse SEO score of 100, and the home page's server-rendered HTML still contains the summary and `Person` structured data.
- Zero Apple trademarked assets or UI-kit material ship.
- The analytics events above fire from launch.
- **Metrics (eng review):**
  - Share attempts are `share_click`. Completed shares are `share_complete`: `navigator.share` resolved, or copy-link succeeded.
  - Every session carries a `first_visit` property (from `hos_seen`).
  - Referred visits are landings that carry the share UTM.
  - The primary metric is completed shares in first-visit sessions divided by first-visit sessions. The secondary metric is referred first-visit sessions per completed share.
  - Targets are set after 2 weeks of baseline data.
